Cortex 2 on the grid

Five stretches of one life of Cortex 2 in the species world, replayed step by step. The forager walks on an 8 × 8 grid that wraps around at its edges. It sees only the object it stands on, and it smells where objects are, but not what they are. At each step it moves one cell or eats what is underfoot. Pick a stretch of its life and press Play.

Poison drought: the rare kinds (the poison, the harm and the rich food, each growing a tenth as often as the others) do not grow in scored segments 1–8. Throwaway stream 99997005, at the settings Cortex 2 had in its development. A probe to look at, not a result.

When the forager eats or leaves an object, a new object, its kind drawn afresh, grows at once in a random free cell, and every object is replaced the same way after about 100 steps on average: so objects jump about the grid.

How this was made

One run of the package's own code, unchanged (pbench.run), in the species world's Poison drought, on the throwaway stream 99997005: Cortex 2 at the settings of Stage 2's confirmation (step size 0.001, entropy bonus 0.09, store weight 1). A youth of 8 segments, then 12 scored segments, each of 20,480 steps. A probe (scripts/probes/cortex2_moves.py) watched the run without changing it and recorded each step in five windows of the life: where the forager stood, every object's cell, role and drawing, the look underfoot, the policy's probabilities, the action and its outcome. The stretches shown here are 400 or 520 steps of those windows; the summary below each counts its whole recorded window.

"Up the smell" is the run's own count. Each corner of the forager's cell reads 1/d² summed over the objects, its own cell's object included; a move goes up the smell when the two corners on its side read more than the other two. Two of the four ways are always up, so a random move goes up half the time. The small chevrons on the forager's cell show the ways up at each step, and the grid's shading is the mean of each cell's four corner readings (a guide; the chevrons are the run's count). (The forager itself senses at the middles of its cell's four edges and leaves out its own cell's object.) In its first 4,000 steps of life, before it had learned much, Cortex 2 went up the smell on of its moves; the reference forager, which knows every species, eats every food it stands on and nothing else, and otherwise always takes the steepest way up.

Each object is drawn as its Omniglot character. The forager never sees that clean drawing: the eye gets it with everyday variation (a slight turn, part of it covered, a haze and noise), as shown under Underfoot.
